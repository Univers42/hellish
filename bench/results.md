# Benchmark results: hellish vs bash vs dash

Generated by `make perf`.  Methodology and every fairness choice: [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md).

Environment: Linux-7.0.0-27-generic-x86_64-with-glibc2.43, CPU governor `powersave` ⚠ **not** `performance` — absolute numbers are pessimistic and noisier; the CV column is the reliability signal.

Speedup columns: that shell's median divided into the reference shell's median — `2.0×` means twice as fast as the reference. CV = stddev/median; results with CV > 3% are flagged ⚠ unreliable.

## Startup

A shell is born, runs `true`, exits.

| benchmark | shell | median | stddev | CV | vs dash | vs bash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| startup | **hellish** | 103.54 ms | 1.62 ms | 1.6% | 1.00× | 1.00× |
| startup | bash | 103.16 ms | 0.65 ms | 0.6% | 1.00× | 1.00× |
| startup | dash | 103.26 ms | 0.79 ms | 0.8% | 1.00× | 1.00× |

## Configure completion note

These shells were **excluded from the configure timing** because they do not complete GNU autoconf `configure` (no `config.status` produced) -- timing a fast failure against a real run would mislead:

- hellish (does not complete configure: no config.status)
